\section{Strategy and headline model}
The strategy is a passive market maker on Kalshi's multi-outcome markets. Every hour it considers posting a buy or
sell limit order (10 contracts) on each leg, and a learned model decides which orders to post: an order is posted
only if its predicted net profit after fees exceeds 1\,c per contract. The headline model, \textbf{G2}, is a
gradient-boosted tree model on 14 market features (price, spread, recent trades, basket structure, fees,
category), retrained on all data up to August 2026. Its predecessor \textbf{G} (trained on Nov 2024--May 2025)
passed a pre-registered test on Aug--Nov 2025 (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). G2 was trained on 23 market series;
the live test covers every series the strategy quotes (290 non-weather series plus the daily temperature
series). Weather markets were first excluded on one validation month, then re-included after a pre-registered check
over six walk-forward months (weather +1.60\% per month on average, positive in 5 of 6; other markets +4.94\%).
The model's edge on weather declined in August--September (net per contract on its picks +1.96\,c in July,
+0.28\,c in August, $-0.99$\,c in September, while the market itself was no harder), so weather and non-weather
results are also reported separately. All rules, models and statistics were fixed and timestamped publicly before
any live result.

\section{Live paper-trading setup}\label{sec:infra}
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{%
\textbf{No knowledge of the future at any step.} This is live paper trading, not a replay: the forward-test
result is the ledger as recorded hour by hour during the test, never a recomputation after the fact.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Models} were trained and frozen before live trading (their hashes are public); nothing is retrained
    or adjusted during the test.
  \item \textbf{Decisions} for each hour use only data available at the live market snapshot taken at the start
    of that hour (about two minutes in); only trades \emph{after} the snapshot can fill the hour's orders. Each
    decision is timestamped when made, and results are also reported counting only trades after that timestamp.
  \item \textbf{Fills} for each hour are determined only from Kalshi trades that occurred \emph{during} that hour,
    after it has ended; no later trade, price or outcome is used.
  \item \textbf{Profit} becomes realised only when a market actually settles; until then, open positions are valued
    at the latest recorded market mid-price.
  \item \textbf{Every record is timestamped} on Lumid Research Fleet shortly after its hour, contains only that
    hour's data, and is never revised afterwards, so no result can be adjusted with hindsight.
\end{itemize}}}
\medskip

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Live decisions.} A producer on a Lumid sandbox scores every candidate order each hour from Kalshi's
    live data and records every candidate's features and the main models' scores before outcomes are known; the
    baseline rules and models are applied to these same logged features. Each hour's record is posted to Lumid
    Research Fleet with a SHA-256 hash, giving an independent timestamp.
  \item \textbf{Live fills, in hourly batches.} The strategy itself is hourly: it posts its orders once per hour,
    and they rest until the end of that hour. Fills are therefore processed in the same hourly batches. Shortly
    after each trading hour ends (typically within 1--2 hours), a paper trader determines that hour's fills
    \emph{only from the Kalshi trades that occurred during it}, records them and timestamps them; they are never
    revised. Because an hour's fills depend only on that hour's trades, processing them trade by trade would give
    the same fills, apart from rare cases where a position limit is reached within the hour. The fill rules are the
    same as the backtest's:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item an order joining the best price fills only if real trades go \emph{through} its price (others are ahead
        in the queue); an order that improves the price by 1\,c fills on trades at its price;
      \item position limits (50 contracts per leg, 200 per event), Kalshi maker fees, full collateral;
      \item positions are held to settlement and valued at the actual results.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Append-only ledger.} Each hour's fills are written to a ledger entry that is never revised. Entries
    are hash-chained (each contains the previous entry's hash). Every round re-checks the recorded fills of markets
    active in the last four days against a recomputation; any mismatch is logged, never overwritten. Settlement
    profit is added as markets resolve.
  \item \textbf{Hourly reporting.} Each hour, for every strategy, Research Fleet receives the
    \emph{cumulative} realised P\&L (settled markets), unrealised P\&L (open positions at the latest recorded mid),
    total P\&L, capital committed and return (pessimistic fills; the other fill assumptions are kept in the ledger),
    plus the ledger entry's hash. Hourly P\&L is the change in total P\&L.
  \item \textbf{Fill assumptions.} Pessimistic fills (above) decide the verdicts; optimistic fills (trades at our
    price also count) and strict fills (competitors match our price improvements) are reported as bounds.
  \item \textbf{What counts.} Only hours recorded live, from the first ledger entry onwards, count towards the
    forward-test result. The hours before the start (11--14 October), when decisions were live but fills were not
    yet recorded live, are shown separately for reference only and do not count. The interim cut for the report is
    28 October; quoting ends 11 November.
  \item \textbf{Verdicts (pre-registered, one-sided 5\%).} (a) G2 is profitable live: the lower 95\% bound of its net
    return is above zero; (b) G2 adds value: G2 minus random selection at the same volume is above zero. Confidence
    intervals are bootstrapped by event (and by day, as a sensitivity check).
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textbf{Checks done.} The paper trader reproduced exactly the 1.1 million orders the producer logged
(0 mismatches); two blind test rounds in the production sandbox (no P\&L viewed) confirmed the ledger, the hash
chain, re-verification and an unchanged producer.

\section{Why G2 stays the headline model (and why not linear model)}\label{sec:linear}
The linear model shows a higher return on capital because it trades less: in the 2025 test it posted 13.5\% of
candidate orders against 19.7\% for G, and had about half as many fills (18,305 vs 34,086). G earns more total profit (2025: +\$3,748 vs +\$2,427; 2026: more profit
in every month), and their returns do not differ significantly. When both models post the \emph{same} number of
orders, G2 earns more than both the linear model and the neural network at every volume tested in 2026. In 2025, G
leads at the volume it actually trades (about 20\% of candidates: +5.30\% vs +4.59\% linear and +4.36\% neural);
the models converge at very high volumes, which I interpret as the easier 2025 market rather than overlapping
picks (overlap rises similarly in both years). G2 is therefore kept as the headline model.

\paragraph{Why the settings were not tuned.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The possible gain is small.} The best of the fourteen boosted settings beat the default by
    0.2 percentage points per month (+2.91\% vs +2.71\%), much less than G2's own month-to-month variation
    (+1.95\% to +3.46\%). A difference this small cannot be told apart from noise over five months.
  \item \textbf{The best setting's lead is partly luck.} Choosing the top scorer among many settings inflates its
    apparent advantage (Cawley \& Talbot, 2010); on new data the expected gain is smaller still.
  \item \textbf{Tuning carries overfitting risk in finance.} Each extra search on short, noisy, changing financial
    data is another chance to fit noise (Bailey et al., 2017; Arnott, Harvey \& Markowitz, 2019). With a negligible
    expected gain, fixed settings are the safer choice; where tunability is low, defaults are appropriate
    (Probst, Boulesteix \& Bischl, 2019).
  \item \textbf{Tuning the neural network could not change the comparison.} Its best setting is about 1.2 points
    behind G2 and still loses money in two of six months; tuning chooses among such settings. On the broader 2025
    data the default network does better (+2.11\%) but G remains significantly ahead (+1.43, CI [+0.47, +2.38]).
  \item \textbf{Fixed settings keep the tests credible.} Settings were recorded before any test; changing them
    after seeing results would weaken the pre-registered tests for almost no expected gain.
\end{enumerate}
Reporting results across all reasonable settings, rather than a single tuned best, follows the logic of
specification-curve analysis (Simonsohn, Simmons \& Nelson, 2020). \textbf{Limitation:} settings were varied one at
a time, so combinations were not searched; a joint search is left as future work. The training window was chosen
by a six-month walk-forward (all history +2.65\% per month vs 3-month +1.53\% and 6-month +1.88\%).
