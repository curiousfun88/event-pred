\vspace{-2em}

\noindent A learned market-making policy (a gradient-boosted model) made \textbf{+3.55\% net of fees} on the
held-out test months (Aug--Nov 2025), with a 95\% confidence interval of +2.51\% to +4.60\% over 34,086
simulated fills. Adding the STGAT's forecasts to it did not improve it. I would like your view on the proposed
next step before continuing.

\section{Headline result}

The learned policy passed its pre-registered verdict (pessimistic-fill return above 0, with the event-clustered
95\% CI excluding 0). Every strategy below was run once on the test months with its settings frozen in advance.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The model does the work.} The same set of quotes posted without the model loses money; the
        model's selection of which quotes to post makes +\$3,748.
  \item \textbf{It held up out of sample.} Validation (Jun--Jul 2025) gave +3.73\%; test gave +3.55\%. The earlier
        rule-based maker (A10) fell from +21.7\% to +1.8\% over the same step.
  \item \textbf{It trades at scale.} 34,086 fills, against 83 for the rule-based maker, so the result does not
        hinge on a few events.
\end{itemize}

\section{How it was tested}

The policy predicts, for each candidate maker quote, its net P\&L per contract at settlement after fees, and posts
the quote only if the prediction exceeds $1\cent$.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Data and split:} Kalshi MECE markets, Nov 2024 -- Nov 2025, split 7 / 2 / 4 months (train Nov--May,
        validation Jun--Jul, test Aug--Nov), as you approved.
  \item \textbf{Model:} scikit-learn \texttt{HistGradient\allowbreak{}Boosting\allowbreak{}Regressor} (histogram gradient boosting,
        LightGBM-style), trained on 163,128 simulated fills from the training months only. Inputs are 14 features
        known at the quoting hour: order side and price, spread, mid, recent trades, 24-hour price change, basket
        context (basket size, sum of mids, best other leg), hour, fee status and category.
  \item \textbf{Simulator:} a tape-driven replay of real Kalshi trades and hourly quotes, with inventory limits and
        Kalshi's fee schedule. Fills are bounded by three queue-position models: optimistic,
        \textbf{pessimistic (the pre-registered verdict)} and strict (competing makers also improve their price).
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration:} the model, features, threshold, validation gate and verdict were committed
        before any test-month run; the frozen model is hash-checked before scoring.
  \item \textbf{Leak checks:} with shuffled training labels the edge disappears ($+0.05\cent$ per contract); it
        survives removing time-to-close, fee status and category, and excluding sports markets (whose recorded
        close time is the actual match end).
  \item \textbf{Validation gate inside the simulator:} +3.73\% (CI +2.43\% to +5.03\%) on Jun--Jul 2025, before the
        single test run.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The test months were used earlier for the rule-based maker; this is disclosed, and no design choice for
the learned policy used them.

\section{Where the STGAT adds value}

The STGAT forecasts basket prices well, but its forecasts carry almost no information for trading beyond simple
market features.

\midrule
Forecasting basket prices & \textbf{\boldmath Beats persistence ($0.635\times$ MSE) and the shrinkage oracle ($0.922\times$)}
  & Anticipates convergence to \$1 as baskets settle \\
Next-hour signal for the rule-based maker (S4/S5) & No improvement; the filter never fired
  & The maker's P\&L is decided at settlement; even perfect next-hour foresight adds about \$1 \\

  & Signal inflated in-sample: correlation with later price moves 0.062 on training months, 0.016--0.025 out of sample \\

\end{center}

\noindent This answers the thesis question directly: the learned model adds value in forecasting basket prices,
but the trading profit comes from execution (which quotes to post given spread, favourite bias and informed flow),
which a model on simple market features captures.

\section{Limitations}

The main caveat is competition: under the strict fill model the edge is not significant.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Strict fills:} if competing makers also improve their price, the learned policy's return falls to
        +0.39\% (CI $-0.96\%$ to $+1.78\%$). The claim is ``profitable under pessimistic fills'', with strict reported
        as the bound.
  \item \textbf{Backtest, not live trading:} orders were never placed. Fills are inferred from historical trades;
        market impact, faster competitors and larger order sizes are not modelled.
  \item \textbf{Hourly resolution:} the simulator re-quotes once an hour; a live maker reacting in seconds could be
        picked off more often.
  \item \textbf{Reused test months:} Aug--Nov 2025 had already been used for the rule-based maker. Disclosed; no
        learned-policy choice used them.
  \item \textbf{Short window:} 13 months in total, two of them validation; one regime of a fast-growing market.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed next step: a model benchmark}

I propose a small, pre-registered benchmark: do state-of-the-art models beat the gradient-boosted policy on the
same task? Estimated 2--4 days, leaving about two and a half weeks for writing.

they show. Deep reinforcement-learning market makers are left out: too data-hungry and unstable for the time left.
